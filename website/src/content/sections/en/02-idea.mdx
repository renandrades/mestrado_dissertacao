## Genes as points in a network

Instead of looking at each gene in isolation, this dissertation starts from a different
question: what if a gene's position inside the protein interaction network already tells
us something about its role in cancer?

In protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks, each gene is a node and each known
interaction between their proteins is an edge. Cancer driver genes tend to interact more
with other relevant genes — a signal that goes unnoticed when a gene is analyzed on its
own, outside of its biological context.

This is where Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) come in: neural networks capable of
propagating information between neighboring nodes, learning a representation for each
gene that combines its own molecular attributes with the context of its neighborhood in
the network.
