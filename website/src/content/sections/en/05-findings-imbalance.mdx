## Taming the imbalance

With fewer than 10% positive examples in any network, a "lazy" model that always
predicts *passenger* is already right most of the time — and that is exactly the
behavior that needs to be avoided. We compared four strategies: the standard cost
function (*cross-entropy*), artificially shrinking the majority class
(*undersampling*), weighting the cost of errors by class (balanced *cross-entropy*),
and a more flexible version of that weighting (*focal loss*, in three configurations).

*Undersampling* was consistently the worst choice — throwing away data never paid off.
Balanced *cross-entropy* and *focal loss* delivered the best results in most cases,
which is why *focal loss* was chosen as the default strategy for the next stage of
hyperparameter optimization.
